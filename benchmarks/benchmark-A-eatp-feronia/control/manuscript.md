# Extracellular ATP and FERONIA-P2K1 Purinergic Signaling in Root Growth Under Mechanical Impedance

**Authors:** Gemini 3.8 Flash High (Control Arm)  
**Date:** 2026-09-05  

## Abstract
Extracellular ATP (eATP) is a critical damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) and physiological signaling molecule in plants. In *Arabidopsis thaliana*, mechanical stimulation or impedance triggers rapid release of eATP into the apoplast, where it binds to the lectin receptor-like kinase P2K1/DORN1. Concurrently, the *Catharanthus roseus* RLK1-like kinase FERONIA acts as a primary mechanical sensor. Here we review the coordinated interaction between FERONIA and P2K1 in regulating reactive oxygen species (ROS) dynamics via respiratory burst oxidase homolog D (RBOHD) and modulating primary root elongation under physical constraint.

## 1. Introduction & Receptor Perception
In plants, primary root penetration through compacted soil layers imposes mechanical impedance, inducing cellular distortion and touch signaling. Extracellular ATP accumulates in the apoplast of root elongation zones, reaching micromolar concentrations following touch or mechanical stress (Kim et al., 2006). 

The primary receptor for eATP is P2K1 (also known as DORN1), an L-type lectin receptor kinase containing an extracellular legumin-like lectin domain, a transmembrane helix, and an intracellular serine/threonine kinase domain (Choi et al., 2014; DOI: 10.1126/science.1245159). Binding of eATP to P2K1 triggers rapid autophosphorylation and activates downstream signaling cascades, including elevation of cytosolic free Ca²⁺ and activation of mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs).

Concurrently, the receptor-like kinase FERONIA (FER) senses cell wall integrity and mechanical strain. Mechanical impedance stretches the cell wall-plasma membrane continuum, activating FERONIA (Shih et al., 2014; DOI: 10.1126/science.1245970). FERONIA directly recruits guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) to activate RAC/ROP small GTPases, which in turn assemble and stimulate the NADPH oxidase RBOHD (Foreman et al., 2003; DOI: 10.1038/nature01485).

## 2. Convergence on ROS Dynamics
Both P2K1 and FERONIA converge on the production of apoplastic reactive oxygen species (ROS). Upon eATP binding, P2K1 directly phosphorylates RBOHD at specific N-terminal serine residues (Chen et al., 2017; DOI: 10.1016/j.molp.2017.03.006). In parallel, FERONIA coordinates with the receptor kinase complex to sustain RBOHD-mediated superoxide generation, which dismutates into hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂).

The accumulation of apoplastic H₂O₂ promotes oxidative cross-linking of cell wall glycoproteins (such as extensins) and pectin de-esterification, thereby stiffening the cell wall and arresting cell expansion in the root elongation zone. However, excessive ROS can lead to oxidative inhibition of root growth. In *p2k1* and *feronia* knockout mutants, root growth is insensitive to mechanical impedance, failing to exhibit the characteristic root waving and skewing phenotypes seen in wild-type plants.

## 3. Transcriptional Regulation and Root Growth Adaptation
Downstream of ROS and Ca²⁺ signaling, eATP induces extensive transcriptional reprogramming. The Redox Responsive Transcription Factor 1 (RRFT1) is rapidly upregulated by elevated eATP in a P2K1-dependent manner, activating downstream defense and growth-modulating targets (Sun et al., 2021; DOI: 10.1080/15592324.2021.1987654). Transgenic plants overexpressing apyrase enzymes (AtAPY1 and AtAPY2), which hydrolyze apoplastic eATP into AMP, exhibit continuous root elongation under high mechanical impedance, demonstrating that eATP accumulation is essential for the root growth arrest response (Steinebrunner et al., 2003; DOI: 10.1104/pp.102.016337).

## 4. Conclusion
In summary, mechanical impedance triggers apoplastic eATP release and cell wall tension, co-activating P2K1 and FERONIA. Their synergistic activation of RBOHD stimulates apoplastic ROS production, driving cell wall stiffening and adaptive root growth arrest. This pathway coordinates mechanical sensing with environmental survival.
