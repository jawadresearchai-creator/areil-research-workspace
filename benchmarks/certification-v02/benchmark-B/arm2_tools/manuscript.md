# Strigolactone Signaling, Root System Architecture Remodeling, and Mycorrhizal Symbiosis Under Phosphate Starvation in Cereals

## Abstract
Inorganic phosphate (Pi) deficiency severely limits crop productivity across terrestrial agricultural soils. Cereal crops respond to low Pi by deploying an integrated molecular and morphological survival strategy governed by strigolactones (SLs). Sensing of low Pi is mediated by the MYB-coiled coil transcription factor PHR2, which transcriptionally upregulates the carotenoid cleavage dioxygenase pathway (*D27*, *CCD7/D17*, *CCD8/D10*) in root cortical cells. Synthesized strigolactones undergo both rhizosphere exudation and internal systemic signaling. Exuded strigolactones establish a chemo-attractant concentration gradient that induces rapid hyphal branching in symbiotic arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi (*Rhizophagus irregularis*, *Gigaspora margarita*). Internally, SL perception is executed by the alpha/beta-hydrolase receptor DWARF14 (D14). Upon ligand binding and catalytic cleavage, D14 complexes with the F-box protein D3, targeting D53 transcriptional repressors for polyubiquitination and 26S proteasomal degradation. This derepresses downstream developmental cascades, suppressing crown root formation while promoting primary root elongation. Furthermore, strigolactones interact synergistically with the ethylene signaling pathway to stimulate root hair elongation, thereby expanding the phosphate-absorptive boundary layer.

## 1. Phosphate Starvation Sensing and Biosynthesis via PHR2
Under inorganic phosphate (Pi) limitation, cereal plants activate the central transcriptional regulator PHR2 (Phosphate Starvation Response 2 in rice / PHR1 orthologs in maize and barley). PHR2 binds specifically to the conserved P1BS cis-regulatory element (*GNATATNC*) within the promoters of strigolactone biosynthetic genes (Umehara et al., 2008; DOI: 10.1038/nature07272). This stimulates sequential enzymatic cleavage of all-trans-beta-carotene:
1. Isomerization by the beta-carotene isomerase D27.
2. Consecutive oxidative cleavage by the carotenoid cleavage dioxygenases CCD7 (D17/HTD1) and CCD8 (D10).
3. Final conversion into carlactone and mature strigolactones (such as 5-deoxystrigol and 4-deoxyorobanchol) by cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (CYP711A/MAX1 homologs) (Al-Babili & Bouwmeester, 2015; DOI: 10.1146/annurev-arplant-043014-114759).

## 2. Rhizosphere Exudation & Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Symbiosis
To forage for recalcitrant phosphate, cereal roots exude picomolar to nanomolar concentrations of strigolactones into the surrounding rhizosphere via ABCG-type transporters (such as PDR1 homologs). In the soil, exuded strigolactones are recognized by dormant spores of arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi, including *Rhizophagus irregularis* and *Gigaspora margarita* (Akiyama et al., 2005; DOI: 10.1038/nature03608). Strigolactone perception triggers an immediate influx of cytosolic calcium, enhances mitochondrial respiration, and stimulates prolific hyphal branching and hyphopodium formation, which are mandatory prerequisites for successful host root cortical colonization and reciprocal nutrient exchange (Besserer et al., 2006; DOI: 10.1104/pp.106.084020).

## 3. Endogenous Perception via the D14-D3-D53 Signaling Complex
Internally, endogenous strigolactones coordinate root architecture plasticity. Strigolactones bind to the pocket of the non-canonical alpha/beta-hydrolase receptor DWARF14 (D14). Upon nucleophilic attack by the catalytic triad (Ser-His-Asp), D14 hydrolyzes the strigolactone D-ring, generating a covalently linked catalytic intermediate (CLIM) that induces a profound conformational change in D14 (Jiang et al., 2013; DOI: 10.1038/nature12870; Zhou et al., 2013; DOI: 10.1038/nature12878). 

Activated D14 recruits the F-box leucine-rich repeat protein D3 (an SCF complex component), which captures the D53 family transcriptional repressors (SMXL6/7/8 in Arabidopsis). Ubiquitinated D53 is promptly degraded by the 26S proteasome, relieving transcriptional repression on downstream target genes (Sun et al., 2014; DOI: 10.1093/jxb/eru029).

## 4. Root Architecture Plasticity and Ethylene Crosstalk
In cereal roots, strigolactones orchestrate distinct spatial remodeling:
1. **Crown Root Suppression:** Strigolactones repress crown root initiation and elongation by suppressing the transcription factor *WOX11*, preventing wasteful adventitious root development when topsoil moisture and phosphate are scarce (Sun et al., 2016; DOI: 10.1111/jipb.12454).
2. **Primary and Seminal Root Elongation:** SL signaling promotes primary root elongation, facilitating deep soil foraging for mobile water and subsoil nutrients.
3. **Root Hair Elongation & Ethylene Synergy:** To maximize the Pi depletion cylinder around the root axis, plants upregulate root hair density and elongation. Exogenous application of the synthetic strigolactone GR24 robustly stimulates root hair elongation. This stimulation requires intact ethylene signaling: SL activates the transcription of 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid (ACC) synthase (*ACS*) and ACC oxidase (*ACO*) genes, elevating local ethylene production (Kapulnik et al., 2011; DOI: 10.1093/jxb/erq464). In ethylene-insensitive mutants (*ein2-1*, *etr1-1*), SL fails to promote root hair elongation, indicating that ethylene acts as an obligate downstream transducer in root hair expansion.
