# Extracellular ATP Perception via P2K1/DORN1 and FERONIA Receptor Kinase in Arabidopsis Roots Under Mechanical Impedance

## Abstract
In plant roots, mechanical impedance stimulates the rapid efflux of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) into the extracellular apoplastic space. In *Arabidopsis thaliana*, extracellular ATP (eATP) functions as a dual physiological regulator and damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP). Apoplastic eATP is recognized by the legume-type lectin receptor kinase P2K1/DORN1, which associates at the plasma membrane with the CrRLK1L receptor kinase FERONIA. Here, we synthesize the molecular mechanism of eATP perception, P2K1-FERONIA receptor kinase heterooligomerization, phosphorylation of the NADPH oxidase RBOHD, downstream transcriptional activation via the Redox Responsive Transcription Factor 1 (RRFT1), and negative feedback regulation by apoplastic apyrases (APY1 and APY2). We critically evaluate the concentration-dependent biphasic effects of eATP on primary root elongation and discuss how physical impedance coordinates chemical and mechanical signaling networks.

## 1. Primary Perception by P2K1/DORN1
Extracellular ATP perception is mediated by P2K1 (Does not Respond to Nucleotides 1 / DORN1), a receptor-like kinase featuring an extracellular L-type lectin domain, a single transmembrane domain, and an intracellular serine/threonine kinase domain (Choi et al., 2014; DOI: 10.1126/science.1245159). Binding of eATP occurs with high nanomolar affinity ($K_d \approx 45.7\,\text{nM}$), inducing receptor dimerization and autophosphorylation within seconds (Cho et al., 2017; DOI: 10.1007/5584_2017_110). Upon ligand binding, P2K1 opens apoplastic calcium-permeable channels, initiating a sharp cytosolic $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ spike and activating downstream mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades (Tanaka et al., 2014; DOI: 10.3389/fpls.2014.00446).

## 2. P2K1-FERONIA Receptor Kinase Complex
Physical impedance and touch stretch the plasma membrane, activating the CrRLK1L family receptor kinase FERONIA, an essential sensor of cell wall integrity and mechanical strain (Shih et al., 2014; DOI: 10.1126/science.1245970). Recent structural and biochemical investigations demonstrate that FERONIA directly forms a heterooligomeric complex with P2K1 at the plasma membrane (Sowders et al., 2024; DOI: 10.1080/15592324.2024.2370706). FERONIA phosphorylates P2K1 and coordinates guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) and RAC/ROP GTPases to amplify downstream purinergic responses.

## 3. Apoplastic ROS Dynamics via RBOHD Phosphorylation
Both P2K1 and FERONIA converge upon the respiratory burst oxidase homolog D (RBOHD). Activated P2K1 directly phosphorylates RBOHD at specific N-terminal residues (Ser343 and Ser347), driving robust generation of apoplastic superoxide ($\text{O}_2^{\bullet-}$), which is dismutated by superoxide dismutases into hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$) (Chen et al., 2017; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-017-02340-3). Elevated apoplastic $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ induces oxidative peroxidase-mediated cross-linking of extensins and cell-wall glycoproteins, rigidifying the cell wall and arresting cell expansion in root elongation zones.

## 4. Transcriptional Reprogramming & Downstream Signaling
Elevated eATP elicits extensive transcriptional reprogramming within 15–30 minutes. The ethylene-responsive and redox-sensitive transcription factor RRFT1 (Redox Responsive Transcription Factor 1) is strongly upregulated downstream of the P2K1-dependent $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and ROS wave (Dong et al., 2020; DOI: 10.1080/15592324.2020.1748282; Chivasa, 2020; DOI: 10.1093/pcp/pcaa033). RRFT1 coordinates defense and secondary metabolic pathways, linking mechanical barrier sensing to plant immunity and growth modulation.

## 5. Biphasic Growth Kinetics and Negative Regulation by Apyrases
Primary root growth responses to eATP follow a strict biphasic dose-response curve:
1. Low physiological nanomolar to low micromolar levels ($< 10\,\mu\text{M}$) promote root viability and cellular elongation.
2. Micromolar concentrations ($> 50\,\mu\text{M}$), typically generated under severe mechanical impedance or crushing, trigger acute thigmo-inhibition and root growth arrest (Weerasinghe et al., 2009; DOI: 10.1016/j.febslet.2009.07.007).

To maintain extracellular ATP within physiological boundaries, plant apoplasts deploy two nucleoside triphosphate diphosphohydrolases: APYRASE 1 (AtAPY1) and APYRASE 2 (AtAPY2). These ecto-enzymes hydrolyze apoplastic ATP and ADP to AMP and orthophosphate (Steinebrunner et al., 2003; DOI: 10.1105/tpc.016337). Double *apy1 apy2* RNAi lines accumulate excessive eATP, resulting in hyper-inhibited primary root elongation and distorted root waving (Yang et al., 2015; DOI: 10.1093/pcp/pcv134; Lim et al., 2014; DOI: 10.1104/pp.113.233429). Conversely, exogenous supplementation of apyrase hydrolyzes excess eATP, preventing sustained RBOHD activation and substantially restoring root elongation under impedance stress.
