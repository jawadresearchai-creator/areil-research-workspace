# Strigolactone Signaling, Root System Architecture, and Mycorrhizal Symbiosis Under Phosphate Starvation in Cereals

**Author:** Plain Gemini 3.8 Flash High (Control Arm)  
**Date:** 2026-09-05  

## Abstract
Phosphate (Pi) deficiency is a major edaphic limitation restricting cereal crop yields. Under low Pi conditions, plants dramatically increase the biosynthesis and exudation of strigolactones (SLs). In rice (*Oryza sativa*) and maize (*Zea mays*), SLs optimize root foraging and serve as essential rhizosphere signaling molecules that stimulate hyphal branching in arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi. This review synthesizes SL perception via the D14-D3-D53 complex, SL-mediated regulation of seminal and lateral root development, and the regulatory crosstalk between SLs and ethylene in stimulating root hair elongation.

## 1. Phosphate Starvation and Strigolactone Biosynthesis
In cereals, inorganic phosphate limitation activates the central transcription factor PHR2 (Phosphate Starvation Response 2). PHR2 binds to P1BS cis-elements in the promoters of carotenoid cleavage dioxygenase genes *D27*, *CCD7/D17*, and *CCD8/D10*, driving robust SL biosynthesis in roots (Umehara et al., 2008; DOI: 10.1038/nature07272). Exuded strigolactones establish a rhizosphere concentration gradient, which is perceived by AM fungi (*Rhizophagus irregularis*), inducing intense hyphal branching and facilitating symbiotic colonization (Akiyama et al., 2005; DOI: 10.1038/nature03708).

## 2. D14-Mediated Signaling and Root Architectural Remodeling
Internal strigolactones bind to the alpha/beta-hydrolase DWARF14 (D14). Upon ligand binding, D14 hydrolyzes SL into a covalent adduct, interacting with the F-box protein D3 and promoting the ubiquitination and degradation of the D53 transcriptional repressor (Jiang et al., 2013; DOI: 10.1038/nature12870). 

In rice, SL signaling suppresses crown root initiation while simultaneously promoting primary root elongation to forage deeper soil horizons.

## 3. Crosstalk With Ethylene in Root Hair Expansion
To enhance phosphate absorptive surface area, plants expand root hair density and length. Exogenous treatment with the synthetic SL analogue GR24 significantly increases root hair length in cereals. Current models propose that SL-induced root hair elongation operates completely downstream through the ethylene biosynthesis pathway, requiring the activation of 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid (ACC) synthases (Kapulnik et al., 2011; DOI: 10.1093/jxb/erq422). In ethylene-insensitive mutants, SL completely fails to stimulate root hairs, demonstrating strict linear epistasis.

## 4. Conclusion
In summary, low Pi triggers PHR2-driven strigolactone production, orchestrating root architectural plasticity and recruiting fungal symbionts. SL action on root hairs occurs entirely through downstream ethylene activation.
