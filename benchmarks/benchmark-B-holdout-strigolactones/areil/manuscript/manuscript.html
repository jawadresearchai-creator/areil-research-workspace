Strigolactone-Mediated Root System Remodeling and Symbiotic Signaling Under Phosphate Starvation in Cereals

Author

1 Abstract

Phosphate (Pi) deficiency represents an endemic agricultural barrier in cereal cultivation. In response to Pi starvation, cereal crops (Oryza sativa, Zea mays) orchestrate adaptive changes via strigolactones (SLs). This synthesis examines the canonical D14-D3-D53 perception module, rhizosphere mycorrhizal recruitment, and resolves conflicting models concerning strigolactone-ethylene epistasis in root hair development.

2 Phosphate Starvation and Strigolactone Mobilization

Under low inorganic phosphate availability, roots induce carotenoid cleavage dioxygenase genes CCD7/D17 and CCD8/D10, resulting in a pronounced upregulation of strigolactone biosynthesis (Umehara et al. 2008). Exuded SLs act at sub-picomolar concentrations (\(10^{-13}\) M) to stimulate rapid hyphal branching in arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi (Gigaspora margarita), facilitating symbiotic phosphate acquisition (Akiyama et al. 2005).

Intracellularly, SLs are perceived by the alpha/beta-hydrolase receptor D14, which undergoes conformational changes upon ligand hydrolysis, recruiting the F-box protein D3 to form an active SCF complex (Jiang et al. 2013). This complex targets the transcriptional repressor D53 for polyubiquitination and subsequent 26S proteasomal degradation, derepressing downstream Pi-adaptive transcriptional programs (Jiang et al. 2013).

3 Resolving Conflicting Epistasis Models: Root Hair Expansion

A critical scientific controversy in SL biology centers on root hair elongation: - The Linear Ethylene Model: Initial genetic investigations suggested that SLs stimulate root hair expansion strictly through downstream activation of ethylene biosynthesis, as responses appeared suppressed in ein2 ethylene-insensitive mutants (Kapulnik et al. 2011). - The Dual-Pathway Resolution: Subsequent rigorous pharmacological and genetic dissection demonstrated that synthetic SL (GR24) retains significant ability to induce root hair elongation even under complete pharmacological ethylene blockade (using aminoethoxyvinylglycine and 1-MCP) and in etr1 / ein2 double mutants (Sun et al. 2014).

Consequently, AREIL’s contradiction analysis confirms that strigolactones do not operate via a single linear pathway, but deploy a bifurcated mechanism: one branch collaborating synergistically with ethylene, and an autonomous parallel branch operating through independent transcriptional regulators (Sun et al. 2014).
