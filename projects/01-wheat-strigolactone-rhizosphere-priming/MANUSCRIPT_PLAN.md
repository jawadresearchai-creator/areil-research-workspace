## Argument Graph
1. **Premise:** Soil phosphorus is severely immobile; individual plants respond via cell-autonomous phosphate starvation signaling (InsP8-SPX-PHR) and exude strigolactones to recruit AM fungi.
2. **Hypothesis:** Belowground broadcast of strigolactones from P-starved wheat roots primes neighboring unstressed wheat, accelerating AM symbiosis before the neighbor experiences nutrient deficiency.
3. **Conduit Dissection:** By separating diffusible solute transport (0.45 um microporous membrane) from fungal hyphal bridging (30 um mesh) and solid barriers (M0), we isolate the chemical cue from fungal-mediated transfer.
4. **Genetic & Chemical Proof:** Priming is abolished by the strigolactone biosynthesis inhibitor Tis-108 in the donor and by loss of the *TaD14* receptor in the recipient.
5. **Agronomic & Ecological Synthesis:** Belowground neighbor priming represents a mutualistic early-warning mechanism, enhancing phosphorus acquisition efficiency in cereal canopies.
